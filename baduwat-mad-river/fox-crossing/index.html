    What this covers

    Twenty dates, March 1942 to October 2025. Every frame is on the same UTM zone 10N grid, so the flip is registered. The drainage view is 3.9 by 3.75 km at 1 m per pixel. The parcel view is 750 m square at 0.3 m per pixel.

    1942 through 1970 are full-resolution scans. These eight dates are mosaics of scanned prints from Cal Poly Humboldt, about 0.5 to 0.8 m per pixel at source. I orthorectified each frame against the 10 m 3DEP elevation model, then tightened each year against the next later year, ending at 2005 NAIP. Offsets between adjacent years measure about 2 to 5 m, more on steep ground. Seams show where flight lines meet, tone differs between prints, and some prints carry grease-pencil lines.

    The two 1942 dates are different flight lines. The March 4 line runs west of the parcel and the July 30 line runs east of it. Both cover the parcel, so the parcel view shows it in late winter and in summer of the same year. In the drainage view each date is filled out with the other line.

    1993 is two USGS frames at about 1.7 m. Chips with dashed borders mark black-and-white dates.

    Color balance varies by year in the NAIP frames and I left the tones as delivered. Canopy texture, openings and road lines are the reliable signal.

    3D views. Each year is draped on the 10 m elevation model and rendered from two fixed cameras. The terrain model is smooth at this resolution, so gullies and road cuts do not show as relief, and steep slopes stretch the photos somewhat.

    Gaps. I found no frames over this drainage between 1970 and 1993, or before March 1942. A November 1941 flight line passes about 2 km to the east. The 1959 and 1960 county flights stop just south of the drainage.

    Parcel boundary is from Humboldt County GIS. The drainage divide is delineated from the 10 m elevation model. Stream lines are from the National Hydrography Dataset. None of these lines is a survey.

    Fire record. The optional fire layer shows CAL FIRE historical perimeters. The Pearson Lumber Co. Fire of September 8, 1956 (350 acres) is mapped across the whole parcel and nearly all of its perimeter lies inside this drainage. Two 1952 fires touch the edges of the drainage. A perimeter drawn dashed burned after the date on screen. Older perimeters were often sketched by hand at coarse scale, and the record misses small and early fires, so treat edges as approximate.

    Canopy height, legacy trees and wetness index

    These three layers are off when the page opens. Turn them on in the legend and set their transparency with the opacity slider. They are available in the drainage, parcel and 3D views and do not change with the date on screen.

    Canopy height is from the 2018 USGS 3DEP LiDAR survey: highest return minus the LiDAR ground surface on a 1 m grid. Ground under 2 m of cover is left clear.

    Legacy trees are LiDAR canopy tops at least 131 ft (40 m) tall with a full crown around the top, the same screen used in the timber survey. It flags 39 tops inside the parcel and 2,664 in the drainage. This is a remote screen and not a cruise. Heights are as of 2018, a top on a steep slope can read tall, and a tree lost since 2018 still shows. The LiDAR tiles I could get stop along a diagonal line in the northeast corner of the drainage, so canopy height and tree flags are blank there and the drainage count is low by that area.

    Topographic Wetness Index is ln(a / tan β), computed from the 10 m elevation model with D-infinity flow routing. It shows where terrain concentrates drainage: swales, hollows and valley floors. It is not a measurement of soil moisture, and at 10 m it does not resolve road drainage or small gullies.
